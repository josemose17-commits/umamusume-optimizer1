# Uma Turn Coach

A turn-by-turn training coach for *Umamusume: Pretty Derby* career scenarios. It covers every scenario on Global now, plus every JP scenario up to the latest, so you can practice ahead of Global.

Open `index.html` in a browser. It needs no build step or server, and it saves your run in the browser.

## How to use it

1. Pick the scenario and your build (distance). Under **Settings**, set the highest failure rate you accept (past it each point costs a lot, so the coach only goes over when every other choice is much worse), your deck's race bonus and max energy. Each new career starts at 100 max energy; outings that raise it (Light Hello's first: +4) add it during the run.
2. Each turn, set energy and mood. For each training, tap the dots for **Cards**, **Rainbow** (friendship) cards and cards with **Bond under 80**, and tick **Hint** if one shows.
3. Optional but recommended: type each training's **Gain** (the green numbers added up) and **Fail %**. Left blank, both are estimated from card counts and energy, and the estimate shows in the box.
4. The coach names the action and any item or buff to use first. It explains why, says what it beat and by how much, and predicts your energy and mood afterwards. Tap any row in **All options** to see its breakdown.
5. The **Run check** box (when a check applies) says whether to keep going or reset the run. See [When to reset](#when-to-reset).
6. If a call looks wrong, open **This call looks wrong?** under it, say why, and copy the report: it has the turn's inputs, deck, tokens and every option's score, so it can be checked and the coach fixed.
7. If the training failed, pick **Failed** (the usual "Get Well Soon!" event: mood -1, -5 to that stat) or **Failed badly** ("Don't Overdo It!", only at 20%+ failure: mood -3, +10 energy with the top choice, -10 to that stat and 2 random stats) under **Training result** before pressing Done. A failed training still uses its energy but gives no stats, bond, tokens or facility progress. Fix the 2 random stats in Stats yourself. If the failure gave you Practice Poor, tick **Got Practice Poor** in the same row (or **Practice Poor** next to Bad condition at any time): while it's on, the coach adds 2% to estimated failure on stat trainings (a failure you type already includes it) and weighs the **Infirmary** (or an outing that cures) against the turns it would cost you. Then press **Done, next turn**. The turn is logged, energy and mood carry forward, and the training inputs reset. If you did something else, press **I did this** on that option. **Undo last** steps back.
8. Under **Stats**, type your current stats and, if you like, your own **Target** for each (left blank, the build's targets apply), and your **Stat priority** (five boxes, most important first: picking a stat for a rank moves it there; **Use the build's order** undoes it). The coach fills stats in that order, each up to its target. Targets and priority carry over to your next career. A stat far below its target counts for more and a stat past it counts less, so the coach steers you toward reaching every target. Under **Sparks ★** type the stars of each blue spark your parents give for that stat (like `3 3 2`): each raises the cap (1 star +4, 2 stars +9, 3 stars +16), and the **Cap** box shows the result. Type the cap your game shows to override it.
9. Mark goal races with **☆ Mark goal race** on their turns. The coach then runs them and plans energy around them. Goal races (career objectives and finale races) cost no energy, except in Trackblazer, where even the debut costs energy; optional races cost about 15. Finale races are marked for you where they are fixed.

Shortcuts: ← and → change turns.

Tap **Turn-by-turn coach** to fold the turn strip, this turn's inputs and the call into one line (the turn and the call) while you use the tabs below. It stays folded or open the next time you visit.

## Your deck and trainee (exact gains)

Open **Trainee and deck** and type your trainee and up to 6 support cards. Names autocomplete from the full database. Then set each card's limit break. From then on:

- each training shows your cards as chips. Tap the ones that appear there, and add **Others** for characters not in your deck;
- tap a card once for "here" and twice for "here with a hint (!)". Friendship (rainbow) is detected from each card's type and bond. If the game shows a different number of rainbows (bonds drift when you start a run partway or miss events), set **Rainbow (as in game)** on that training: it fixes those cards' bonds, so this turn and later ones come out right;
- bonds go up by 7 for each training together and about 5 more for a hint. Card events are logged with the **+5** / **+10** buttons, or by tapping the gauge color the game shows (Blue, Green, Orange 80+, Max);
- Friend and Group cards (Light Hello and others) track outings. Once **Outings unlocked** is ticked, "Outing with ..." is weighed against training as its own option. Each card's **Outings** list (from game8's card pages) shows which event unlocks them and which choice keeps them, then every outing with what each choice gives; the coach uses those values and tells you which choice to pick for this turn (the energy one when you're tired, the stat one when you're not). Covered: SSR Tazuna (both), Riko Kashimoto, Sasami Anshinzawa, Light Hello, SR Aoi Kiryuin, The Throne's Assemblage, Team Sirius (only its last outing is listed), and the JP friend and group cards (Mei Satake, Ryoka Tsurugi, Yayoi Akikawa, Tucker Bryne, Kiyoko Hoshina, Casino Drive, Ancestors & Guides, Carvers of History, Embodiment of Legends). Other cards (the R friend cards) use typical values, for up to 5 outings. Outing values are rescaled to your card's LB (its Event Recovery for energy, Event Effectiveness for stats), an outing that raises max energy raises it in the coach too (Light Hello's first: 104), and with **Track stats** on, outing stats are added to your current stats;
- every card has an **Events** list from the Umamusume Wiki: its event chain and other events, what each choice gives (ranges by LB shown at your card's), and a ★ on the better choice for this turn (the energy choice when you're tired, the stat choice when you're not). About 80% of Global cards have every event's results on the wiki; events without results yet show their title only;
- gains, skill points, energy cost and failure come from the game's training formula with your cards' real effects at their level: stat bonus, friendship, mood effect, training effectiveness, card count, your trainee's growth bonuses, plus energy cost reduction, failure protection and conditional unique effects such as bond-gated bonuses;
- your race bonus is added up from your cards automatically.

Scenario-specific boosts that the formula doesn't cover (Unity training, island facilities, springs and so on) use an estimated multiplier. Type the real total gain on a few turns and the coach corrects it.

**Support cards** and **Trainees** tabs browse the whole database:
- **Support cards**: filter by type, rarity, Global availability, limit break and whether you own the card, and sort by any effect. Tap a card for its effects at every limit break, its unique effect, hint skills, event skills and release dates.
- **Trainees**: aptitudes, growth bonuses and unique skill.

## Career goals

Picking a trainee loads her career goals from GameTora (her objectives list): fixed goal races are marked as goal races on their turns (★ on the turn strip, and the coach races them), "pick one" goals that fall on the same turn (Oaks or Derby) count as a goal race either way, "pick one" goals on different turns show as options you choose by tapping ★ on that turn, and fan or race-count goals show on the turn plan as deadlines. You can unmark any goal turn with the goal button. Scenarios with their own goal races (L'Arc) keep those, Trackblazer keeps only the debut (its goals are Grade Points), and every scenario's finale stays as before.

## Fan goals and races

The **Optional race open** list shows the races on this turn from the career calendar (GameTora) that fit your trainee's aptitudes (B or better), with their expected fans; **Fans** tracks your fan count (type it from the game; Done on a race adds the expected fans, with your deck's Fan Bonus). When a fan goal is coming, the coach plans it with the real races before the deadline, counting your goal races and races you can only enter with more fans: it says **race now** when later races can't cover it (and adds the best race this turn if you didn't pick one), says racing now is **worth it** when it saves you races later or fits the plan for free, and says you can **keep training** when bigger races later do the job, listing them. If the goal can't be reached with races that fit, it tells you to race every chance, since missing a goal ends the run.

## When to reset

At each reset point the **Run check** box in the call answers what the coach already knows (bonds from your deck, songs you've marked, a song ticked in your shop, an actual Light Hello +20 event) and only asks what it can't know (Yes/No, a number or an exam rank). Tap **Not right?** next to an answer to give your own. It decides from the answers:
- **Passed**: keep going.
- **Missed**: **Reset this run**, with two choices: **Reset: start a new career** (tap twice), or **Continue and finish this run** (the coach stops asking about that check). Either way a **Run summary** opens: add a note and press **Copy run summary**, then paste it to me. It has the checks and your answers, stats against targets, stat priority, deck and bonds, songs and tokens, and every turn you logged, so the coach can be fixed. It's offered again when the run ends.

The checks (under **Settings → Reset advice**: **Building an ace (strict)**, the default, **Only when a goal is lost**, or **Off**):
- **Career goals** (every mode): a fan or Grade Point goal that even winning every race that fits you can't reach. A warning first, since races outside your aptitudes can still save it, and a reset with 2 or fewer turns to the deadline.
- **Grand Concert**: on the debut (turn 12), "Is any stat card's bond orange (80+)?" and "Has Light Hello given her +20 event?" (recording the actual event counts as yes; old saved checkmarks still count): no to both is a reset. On turn 23, "How many songs have you bought?" and "Is a 6th song showing, held for after the live?": fewer than 5, or no 6th, is a reset (the CM-ace guide).
- **Mecha**: after each Upgrade Exam (turns 24, 36, 48, 60), "What rank did you get?": an A or lower is a reset (game8).
- **Yukoma Hot Springs**: on turn 21 (Junior November), "How many springs have you dug?": fewer than 2 is a reset (game8).
- **Bonds** (every scenario, ace mode): going into Classic (turn 24), "Are most of your stat cards (60% or more) at friendship?": a no is a warning, never a reset on its own.

## Detailed run reports

Open **Career log → Detailed run report**, or **This call looks wrong? → Copy full run report**. Reports are available even with reset advice off and before any command is logged. **Download run JSON** saves a file for a larger run. The existing reset-check summary uses the same detailed export.

New logged turns retain the recommendation, every ranked option, your actual chosen command, entered training inputs, the recorded result and the command's closing state. Grand Concert adds per-type token accounting, cap losses and progress at each recorded concert. Song and technique purchases made between commands, purchase Undo, observed Hello events, song-history corrections and entered token-balance corrections are included. Formula amounts and assumed token types are identified; expected Hello events never become recorded income. Token corrections remain separate from earned tokens and recorded purchase costs.

Existing saves remain usable. Their original input snapshots are exported where available, while missing decisions, outcomes and purchases stay unknown. The report identifies gaps and partial history instead of reconstructing an unobserved run. Full reporting starts with actions recorded in this version. Reports are generated locally and shared only when you copy or download them. See the [diagnostics audit](docs/grand-concert-run-diagnostics.md).

## My cards

Mark the support cards you own and their limit break, with the **Owned** picker on any card or **Quick add** under **My cards** in the Support cards tab. They're saved in your browser. **Copy backup code** gives a short code (`UMA1:...`) you can keep or paste into **Restore** on another device. When you add a card you own to your deck, it starts at your limit break, and **Cards I own** limits the deck picker to your cards.

Cards reach Global in the data when GameTora lists their Global date (refreshed daily). If a card is out on Global but the data doesn't know yet, open it in **Support cards** (untick **Global only** to find it) and press **Mark as out on Global**: it then counts as a Global card everywhere (deck picker, optimizer, tier list), in this browser. A date from the data takes over once it arrives, and **Unmark** undoes it.

## CM & decks

Your team and each ace's racing deck. Older **CM/LoH** and **CM/LoH planner** saves open here with their settings.

1. **Team:** pick the cup and up to three aces (from My umas). **Use my Pull planner team** copies that track's team. The deck below is for the ace you pick.
2. **Deck:** the deck optimizer as before: running style, deck makeup, friend card, must-include cards, must-have skills, skill weight, Euophrys weights, target stats, rank goal, Independent Training. **Find my best deck** builds it, **Which cards would improve it?** and **Use this deck in the coach** work on it.
3. **Her gaps:** under the predicted stats, each stat's target (yours, or the race build's when blank), the prediction (16 careers, before inheritance) and how far short. **Fill them in Parents** goes to the next step. The skills table for the race stays at the bottom.

Each ace keeps her deck by cup. Racing and parent decks keep separate results.

**The skills table** ranks every skill by its median length gain (L) on the cup's course for her running style, from alpha123's Umalator. The Umalator runs one uma alone, so the values place her in the pack by style (a Front Runner in 1st, a Pace Chaser 2nd–4th, Late Surgers and End Closers 5th–9th of 9) and leave out the skills she can't fire from there: ones that need her in the back half of the pack (for Front Runners and Pace Chasers) or the front half (for End Closers), and, for a Front Runner, ones that need a rival right ahead of her (No Stopping Me!, Blast a Hole Through!, Slipstream, passing skills), since nobody is ahead of a leader. The line under the table says what was left out, and a must-have she can't fire is marked. This applies everywhere the values count: the deck optimizer, must-haves and Parents. Skills that wait for other skills to fire are valued as if those had fired: Groundwork and Solid Preparation (3 skills in the opening leg) go off at the gate, where greens, Concentration and Early Lead fire, so the extra acceleration lands while she's still speeding up (worth most to a Front Runner); Tail Held High and the others at a random moment of their leg. For a Front Runner, Concentration, Focus and greens are tagged (**helps her lead**, **sets off Groundwork**): their L is only the distance a quicker start saves or their own stat boost, but they fire at the gate, where they help her take the lead and, three together, set off Groundwork. The simulator can't put a number on that.

## Parents

Steps 4 to 8 of the plan, top to bottom, for the ace picked in CM & decks. Older **Parents** saves open here.

1. **Her gaps:** CM & decks' negative numbers, what her family's blues already give against them (your veteran, the grandparent once you enter her, Parent 1 once she's trained), and the blue you can still pick: only borrowed umas have a blue you choose (the parent you train gets a random stat), so it tries every split of the blues you still pick (the grandparent's own, Parent 2's own and her two parents', 2★ or 3★ each, at most 8★ in one stat across Parent 2's line) and takes the one that leaves the least short. The searches ask for exactly that split (e.g. Stamina 3★ on Parent 2, Stamina 6–9★ and Power 3–9★ across her line). A gap within 20 (one inspiration roll) shows as ≈ closed; a bigger one says to strengthen the deck or lower the target. The Parent 2 and grandparent searches ask for blue in the biggest gap still open, and **Sparks to aim for** and the 10 skills take her family into account: whites it already gives rank lower, and the pink line says what it already brings.
2. **Make Parent 1:** by default a new parent from My umas, trained from one of your veterans and a borrowed grandparent; **My veteran is Parent 1** is the other choice. She's ranked by what she gives the ace: expected sparks (whites more likely when her parents carry them), the G1s she can share, and affinity. Families that can't reach **250 affinity** aren't suggested. **Grandparent search** opens uma.moe with the new parent as the target, your veteran (and her G1 wins) as the other parent, and filters on the rental's own sparks only (her parents would be the ace's great-grandparents): blue 3★ in the ace's biggest gap, pink 2–3★ by your rule, the race's whites as her own preferred whites. Both characters are left out. Every filter is listed under the link; **Change the search** edits them. Under it, **The grandparent you borrowed**: pick her uma and enter her own sparks (blue, pink, green, the whites that matter here) and G1 wins from her uma.moe card, then **Plan with her**. Or type her **trainer ID** and press **Fill from uma.moe**: it reads her listing from uma.moe's API (her veteran record when the profile has it, with her G1 wins) and fills the form for you to check. uma.moe only answers with your own API key (uma.moe → Settings → API Keys), which stays in your browser. With her in the plan, her sparks count for the ace, her G1s go first in the new parent's races, and the family check uses her.
3. **Train her**, top to bottom in the order it's worked out: **her Ace-parent deck** (a quick build from your cards, or the deck you build in ③: after **Find my best deck** there, **Find again** plans with it), **her extra G1s** with that deck (as many as keep her at SS; with the quick build the deck is rebuilt once for the races she keeps), then the **Independent Training settings** and **Sparks to aim for** for that deck and those races. **Sparks to aim for**: the whites to learn (gold versions double the spark chance), whether her unique counts here, and the odds for pink and blue. Those two are random: blue picks any stat, pink one of her A/S aptitudes, so only their stars can be pushed. Always SS, with as many G1s as keep her at SS (your veteran's first, each shared one adds affinity), her Ace-parent deck and the skills to learn. **Independent Training settings**: the **Training Focus** (Balanced, Stamina or Sprint: her run simulated with each, the most runs at SS or else the highest rating wins; Sprint leans to Power, and how far each leans is an estimate), the **10 prioritized skills** in order (the race's whites her deck can hint or give through events, most valuable as an ace spark × most likely first, the gold version when her deck offers it, any spare slots for whites only a random event could give; a skill one of her events gives on every choice comes anyway, so it's listed apart and takes no slot), and the **agenda** (her G1s). **Set her up to train** fills ① her, ② her races, ③ her deck and ④ the skills to buy.
4. **Parent 2 search:** the ace as the target, Parent 1 (her planned G1s) as the other parent, blue in the gap her family leaves open, pink by your rule, the whites still missing as preferred whites. You pick from uma.moe's results.

**Whites in both searches:** every listed white goes in as a preferred white (P0 for the top three, then P1, P2): uma.moe ranks rentals with them first, then by affinity, and drops none. **Require** any number of them (or **Require all**); each one you tick must be there, so each cuts the results. **Not in the link: affinity only** leaves them out.

**After you train Parent 1:** import your umaextractor export again. Parents finds her (the version you planned, made after the plan or with your veteran as a parent), makes her Parent 1 and rebuilds the Parent 2 search from her real sparks, parents and G1 saddles. **Plan a new parent instead** sets her aside.
5. **Her family:** the affinity the game will show once you borrow (the open slots take the best-affinity characters with the G1s rentals most often win): ✓ at 250+, or how far short.

**Rules it follows.** Blue: each family member gives +5/+12/+21 at the start and can fire at both inspirations (70/80/90% × affinity). Pink: an aptitude still below A that her family doesn't lift comes first, asking for just the stars the start lift needs on top of her family's (1★ for one grade, 4★ for two, 7★ for three; more don't help), then S distance (3–9★). Each uma carries one pink, so the grandparent's search asks one; Parent 2's line (three umas) asks both the below-A aptitude and the S-distance one. Searches and the new parent's skill lists only use whites out on Global today, S surface for a sprint, style up to A; stars lift an aptitude to A at the start, A to S only when a pink fires. Grandparents' sparks fire half as often (an estimate). The family table and the veterans import are at the bottom; **How this works** has the details. See [uma.moe's filter mapping](docs/umamoe-filters.md) and the [Grand Concert model audit](docs/grand-concert-light-hello.md).

## Pull planner

Your plan's banners at 400, and the best use of everything else (other banners, selector picks), so your teams' decks get clearly stronger in as many cups as your carats allow, through mid-2029. It builds your decks for every cup ahead from the cards you own, in the scenario Global trains in then, and puts every support card and trainee on one scale.

- **Your plan**: the banners you'll pull no matter what, each at its # Pulls, from another planner's table (prefilled with the one you sent: Mejiro Ramonu, Mei Satake + El Condor Pasa and Ryoka Tsurugi + Orfevre at 400; Dream Journey's trainee banner and Yayoi Akikawa + Nishino Flower at 200; Still in Love at 400; Daiwa Scarlet + Air Shakur and Orfevre's trainee banner at 200; Embodiment of Legends + Almond Eye, Tucker Bryne + Tamamo Cross and Stay Gold + Admire Groove at 400; Kiyoko Hoshina + Tokai Teio at 200; Casino Drive + Forever Young, Tazuna Hayakawa + Meisho Doto and Mr. C.B. + Efforia at 400). Paste a newer table to replace it. Each row is matched to the calendar by its cards (or its uma, for a trainee row) and date; a step-up isn't modeled. Its **Carat Est.** is that planner's balance at the row, after the pulls of the rows above it (two rows on one day show it), so the budget adds back what those rows cost: the carats you'd have by each date if you spent none, straight lines between, from today at the first stretch's pace or your carats now. Its **Free/Tickets/Paid** are pulls usable on that banner only, spent before carats. The route takes these banners first, in date order, at exactly their # Pulls, and lists any that don't fit, with how many carats short; the carats left over go to whatever else is worth it. On them the sparks go where they add the most, else to the card your table targets (its "Odds for"). A trainee row for one of your team's umas makes her yours from that banner. A row whose banner has ended (your table's End, else the calendar's) is behind you: it's no longer locked, and its carats aren't added back (the balances after it have them paid), so you don't have to paste a new table after every banner.
- **Your selector picks**: the Uma picks you've decided, one per anniversary's $20.99 Uma selector (prefilled: Bamboo Memory at the 2nd Anniversary, Manhattan Cafe at the 2.5 Anniversary). They're taken first; such an uma is yours from that anniversary. Every other pick is the planner's.
- **Your carats** (without a plan): carats now, carats per month, and support and trainee tickets (they pay for pulls on their kind of banner first). The rules are Global's: 150 carats a pull, so 200 pulls (one spark) are 30,000 carats and 400 (two sparks) 60,000. Spark points don't carry over to the next banner.
- **Your future teams**: one team of three per kind of track (Sprint & Mile, Medium & Long, Dirt), each uma with her running style: Bamboo Memory, Ikuno Dictus and King Halo; Orfevre, Dream Journey and Manhattan Cafe; El Condor Pasa, Copano Rickey and Valentine Yukino Bijin. Your team umas are yours from when you'll have them: now (My umas, in the Trainees tab), from your plan (her trainee banner, your selector pick), or else from when she's out on Global. The first two of each team are fixed: each races every cup of her track from then (her slot is a flex before). The third is yours too, but in CM and LoH her slot is a **flex**: each cup, the uma of yours with A or better on its surface, distance and her best running style, a different one from the cup before when another fits. Any slot can be fixed or flex.
- **Banners**: the ones Global has announced, with their own dates and every card's rate (a featured SSR or 3★ trainee 0.75%, an SR or 2★ trainee 2.25%), then GameTora's forecast of the rest. `data/banners.js` is rebuilt daily by `tools/build_banners.py`, with the anniversaries (selectors), the scenarios ahead and the story events' free SSRs.
- **Your decks now**: for every cup up to the last one in the forecast (mid-2029, or 12, 18 or 24 months) and every uma of its team, her racing deck as CM & decks builds it: Euophrys' scoring plus that race's skill values for her style, with its deck makeup, friend card and skill weight. Its cards: yours (at your limit breaks) and the story events' free SSRs at LB4 once their event is over.
- **Scenarios**: each cup's decks are trained in the scenario Global is on by then (GameTora's forecast: Grand Masters from ~Nov 29, 2026, then Project L'Arc, U.A.F. Ready GO!, Great Food Festival, Hashire! Mecha Umamusume, The Twinkle Legends, Design Your Island and three more by mid-2029). Euophrys scores Grand Concert, Grand Masters and Design Your Island (from its JP bundle). For the others it has no preset, so they're estimated: Grand Masters' (or Design Your Island's) weights with the scenario's own friend or group SSR from its opening banner as the link card, at the bonus Euophrys gives Tucker Bryne in Design Your Island. That's what makes a scenario's friend card count, and only while its scenario lasts. Your own Euophrys weight changes apply in the scenario they're for.
- **Clearly stronger, and not a one-time use**: a banner card counts in a deck only when it beats the card it would replace (the "Which cards would improve it?" test, with the other five fixed) by your margin (10% by default), at the limit break your copies reach. A card you'd otherwise borrow counts as yours, with the next-best friend's card. It also has to do that in enough different cups after its banner (2 by default).
- **Longevity**: a card counts in every cup after its banner, and a cup a year ahead counts less by the fade (20% a year by default: forecast uncertainty and newer cards).
- **200 or 400 pulls** (banners outside your plan): 200 pulls on an SSR's banner likely end at LB1, 400 at LB4. A pull counts only when its likely result (each featured card's median copies, plus the sparks) already clears your rules. Its value is the expected points over every result the pulls can give, with the sparks where they help most once you see the pulls, the other featured cards' copies included. Points are the deck score's units (1 L of race skills = 60).
- **Selectors**: at each anniversary, JP's campaigns on Global's calendar. Each has a free support selector and the $20.99 support and Uma selectors (uma.guide's selector overview); tick the ones you'll get. The pool: the gacha SSRs (or 3★ umas) JP had about 45 days before that anniversary. A pick is one copy, valued like a pulled one.
- **Parents and supports on one scale**: a trainee counts as Parent 1 for your fixed team umas (yours to train; Parent 2 can be a friend's rental). Her sparks fire at their base chance × (1 + her affinity with the ace ÷ 100) at the ace's two inspirations, as in the Parents tab. Trained for the ace, she carries the ace's top 3 race whites and her inherited unique at 2★, and a 2★ pink spark where the ace lacks an A on the race (like El Condor Pasa's dirt B) and she has it. Blue sparks are left out: any parent can carry them. All of it is in the same points as a deck card, so trainee and support banners compete for the same carats. **Every banner on one scale** lists each banner on its own (200 and 400 pulls, and per 1,000 carats), your plan's marked, with the best trainee against your plan's weakest support. Characters not on Global yet take their affinity from JP's table (`data/parent-data.js` affinityJp).
- **The route**: your plan's banners first, then step by step the move with the most points per carat that still leaves enough carats at every banner (free selector picks first, then pulls). After each step, the decks take the step's likely cards, so the next card has to beat those. If a pull came after a selector pick, a second pass replays the pulls in date order and chooses the picks again on top of them, so no pick goes to a card a pull already brings to LB4. The table shows only what's outside your fixed plan: the selector picks left to the planner (the free and $20.99 support selectors, and any Uma selector you left blank) and the banners your leftover carats go to. Your plan's banners and Uma picks fold into **Your fixed plan in the route** under it, with the same columns. Each row shows when, the cost (carats, free pulls, tickets), the card it sparks for at its likely limit break (with the chances of every other result), the other featured cards' copies that come with the pulls, the cups and decks it makes clearly stronger, the carats left (your fixed plan's pulls before it included) and what it adds; a selector pick also lists the next best picks there. **What I got** (on every row): once you've pulled or picked, enter your limit break now for each featured card (or tick the umas you got) and save. It goes to My cards (My umas), the banner is marked behind you (its carats come off the budget if it wasn't in your plan) and the route plans again from there; Undo puts it back. **Check my plan against the planner's own route** plans the same carats and free pulls with none of your banners locked (the ones it can't value stay as you planned) and shows the total points of both and the banners that differ. Below are the selectors with no pick. Each support one gets a **pick anyway**, planned together the way the route plans: each pick goes in with the ones before it in your decks, so a card's next copy is worth what its next limit break adds (never past LB4), and a card that takes an earlier pick's spot is worth what it adds over that one. A card worth the same at an earlier or a later selector (its points all come after both) goes to the later one, leaving the earlier one for a card that helps sooner. Each line gives the limit break it takes the card to, its points (rules aside) and the first cup it adds to; the route's decks are put back after, so none of this changes the route. ## How the coach decides

Every option is scored in value points: stats, skill points, bond building, hints, scenario bonuses, energy, mood and failure risk. A score of 100 means a typical training for that point in the career.

- **Stats**: each training raises the stats it really raises (for example Speed training gives Speed plus Power), split by the scenario's base training values. They're weighted by your build. With current stats entered, a stat far below its target counts for more (the same "need" the deck optimizer uses), and stats past your target, past 1200 or at the cap are worth less or nothing. Since the July 2026 update a stat past 1200 gains only half from training and counts about half in races, so both are applied. Enter current stats under **Stats and caps** to switch this on, and they update as you press Done.
- **Focus cards** (with your deck set): getting one card to friendship (bond 80, the orange gauge) early is a must, and a second is nice to have. The coach picks them by an early-rainbow score from each stat card's current bond and specialty priority: **score = (100 + SP) / (550 + SP) × 0.92^T × (1 + Friendship bonus) × (0.75 + 0.25 × stat weight)**. The first part is how often the card is on its own training, where it rainbows (each training weighs 100, its own 100 + its Specialty Priority, not showing up 50, from fan guides). T is the turns to orange: the trainings it still needs ((80 − bond) / 7, rounded up) divided by how often it's on some training ((500 + SP) / (550 + SP)) × 0.6 (about how often you can follow it). 0.92^T makes each turn of waiting cost about 8%, since early rainbows matter most. So a card close to orange wins until a higher-specialty card is only a little behind, and training with your focus card keeps it on top. With no card orange yet, the best one is **Focus 1 (must)**: every training it's on gets a big push (full to the debut on turn 12, fading out by the end of Junior year). The next best is **Focus 2**: a small push that only tips close calls and never outweighs extra song tokens. Once one card is orange, the best of the rest is Focus 2. Hover a Focus chip in your deck to see its numbers. Friend and Group cards aren't focus cards, and past 80 the coach doesn't count a card's bond.
- **Energy**: a lookahead plans the rest of the career, taking into account summer camps, goal races (which cost no energy) and how failure climbs as energy drops. A rest is recommended only when the energy is worth more later than this turn's training. Energy has no value on the last turn.
- **Mood**: one mood step is worth about 10% on upcoming trainings, and more just before a goal race.
- **Deck strength**: after 3 or more turns with real gains entered, the coach learns how strong your deck is compared with a typical one and rescales its estimates. With your deck set, the gains you type also teach it how big the scenario's own bonuses are (Settings shows the result). The game shows gains past 1200 already halved, so the coach halves its estimate the same way before comparing. Type the **total** (every green number added up); a number that's only the training's own stat is spotted and read as that. The learned multiplier stays within 0.7x-1.6x of the formula, so a few slips can't halve every estimate. **Forget learned deck strength** in Settings clears both.
- **Late in a run**: once stats pass 1200 (half the gain, and half the value in races) or their targets, future trainings are worth less, so the energy and mood that buy them are worth less too. The coach scales them down by how much your stats can still gain, so it doesn't rest on a good training late in the run to save energy for trainings that would add little.
- **Scenarios**: each scenario adds its own rules, such as Spirit Bursts and Extreme bursts, Megaphones, Ankle Weights and Charms, Goddess Wisdom colors, SS Matches, Heat-Ups, dishes, Overdrive, legend buffs, Island Training, baths and PR activity, DREAMS training, and tasting sessions.

Cards it doesn't list yet use its fallback (+5 bond, +7/+9 to every stat for SR/SSR) and are marked as estimates. Each deck slot shows its card's total event bond.

`data/gametora.js` holds 563 support cards (effects at every level, unique effects, hint and event skills, JP and Global release dates), 270 trainees (aptitudes, growth bonuses, skills) and the skill names they reference. It comes from [GameTora](https://gametora.com/umamusume). Effect values at each level use a port of GameTora's own interpolation, so they match its card pages. Base training values per scenario come from GameTora's scenario pages. Onsen, Beyond Dreams and Trecen-ken haven't been published yet, so they use Island's values as a stand-in.

To refresh the data (needs network access to gametora.com):

```
python3 tools/build_data.py
```

The generated file is committed, so the coach works offline.

**Automatic updates:** `.github/workflows/update-data.yml` runs every day (and on demand from the Actions tab). It rebuilds the GameTora and Euophrys data, and when there are new cards, trainees, Global release dates or event values and the tests pass, it commits them to `main`, bumps the `?v=` cache version and asks GitHub Pages to rebuild. Cards and trainees also switch to "on Global" by themselves on their Global release date. Champions Meeting dates and skill values still need a manual rebuild (see below), since they depend on the Umalator's files.

Champions Meeting and League of Heroes presets (`data/presets.js`) come from GameTora's event lists plus alpha123's Umalator preset lists:

```
python3 tools/build_presets.py <GameTora cache dir> <umalator-global bundle.js> <umalator bundle.js> <umalator course_data.json> presets.json
```

Skill values (`data/skillvalues/`) come from running alpha123's [Umalator](https://alpha123.github.io/uma-tools/umalator-global/) headless: `tools/umalator_run.js` loads its `simulator.worker.js` in Node and uses the website's skill-chart sampling, with a fixed stat line for each distance. Global cups through CM21 use Global's current simulator. CM22 onward and League of Heroes use a simulator with each cup's JP-dated skill versions, never older than Global's current versions, and that date's supported stamina-showdown rules (see the CM & decks section). uma-tools is GPL-3.0; its code is downloaded at build time, and only the resulting numbers are committed. To rebuild, first prepare the simulator variants and input files as described in `docs/skill-values-handoff.md`, then run:

```
node tools/build_skillvalues.js presets.json <umalator-global dir> <umalator dir> <cache dir> --workers=4 --variants=<variants dir>
node tools/build_skillvalues.js presets.json <umalator-global dir> <umalator dir> <cache dir> --compose=data/skillvalues --gametora=<GameTora cache dir> --variants=<variants dir>
node tools/build_count_skills.js <umalator-global dir> <umalator dir> <GameTora skills.json> --workers=4 --variants=<variants dir>
node tools/build_skillvalues.js presets.json <umalator-global dir> <umalator dir> <cache dir> --compose=data/skillvalues --gametora=<GameTora cache dir> --variants=<variants dir> --metadata-only
```

The last pass refreshes names and JP release dates for skills inserted by the count-skill pass. It preserves those skill values and each cup's simulator provenance. Simulation jobs are cached, so an interrupted rebuild can resume.

When releasing a change to the site, bump the `?v=` number on the file links in `index.html` so browsers fetch the new files instead of cached ones.

## Limits

These are estimates, not a game simulator. Support card event effects use approximate values (bond +5 per hint or outing). Friend and Group outings use game8's values at the card level game8 lists (usually full limit break, so a lower-LB card gives a little less); outings that roll a great success or can fail are counted half and half, since the odds aren't published. Card effects and level-1 base training values come from GameTora's data. The increase per facility level, rest amounts, race rewards and scenario-specific bonuses are approximations. Real gains and failure rates from your screen always beat the built-in estimates. Turns marked "approx." are best-effort, so trust the in-game goal list when they disagree. The newest JP scenarios are based on launch-period guides.

The deck optimizer ranks decks with a simplified career simulation, not a full game simulator: card events, outings and scenario-specific mechanics are approximate, so treat close results (a few percent apart) as ties. Skill values are for one fixed stat line per distance with no other skills, like the Umalator's default chart. Your own uma's numbers can differ, which is what the paste box is for. Global dates past the announced cups are estimates.
